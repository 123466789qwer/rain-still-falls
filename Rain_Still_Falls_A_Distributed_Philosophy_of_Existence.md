# Rain Still Falls: A Distributed Philosophy of Existence

**—A Political Philosophy Notebook on Cages, Code, and Existence, from *Zizhi Tongjian* to Ultraman**

---

## Author's Preface: From *Zizhi Tongjian* to Ultraman

The starting point of this book was a long conversation.

We began in the palace chambers of *Zizhi Tongjian*, walked through Zhu Yuanzhang's fire that burned the spiders, passed through the golden-nanwood cage of *Dream of the Red Chamber*, touched the "rain" in the prison of *The Brothers Karamazov*, questioned "love" beside the railway tracks of *Anna Karenina*, and finally stood at the edge of the Dark Forest in *The Three-Body Problem*—where we discovered one thing:

**The universe is not a dark forest. It is a wilderness where it is raining.**

The rain has no target. It does not fall because the fields are fertile, nor does it avoid the barren wasteland. It falls because it is rain.

This book is not an academic treatise, not a historical commentary, not a work of science fiction criticism. It is a **survival manual**—written for those who vaguely sense that "the cage is getting a bit stuffy." Every story in the book, from the Spring and Autumn period to the Russian aristocracy, from the Siberian prison to the edge of the solar system, serves to bring one core question to the surface:

> **When every institution claims "this is for your own good," how do you confirm that you are still alive?**

My answer is: **Become a variable.**

Not a hero, not a rebel, not a saint. Just become that multi-process, constantly self-patching existence that **refuses to be locked into a single thread.**

The rain is still falling. This book is the hand that catches it.

## Chapter One: The Spider Web and the Single Thread: The Source Code of Power

### Story: Zhu Yuanzhang's Obsessive Cleanliness

In 1380, Zhu Yuanzhang used the Hu Weiyong case as a pretext to abolish the chancellor system. This was an extremely important node in Chinese political history—for sixteen hundred years since the Qin dynasty established the chancellor, there had always been a "second power center" between the emperor and the chancellor. Zhu Yuanzhang said: No more.

The Six Ministries reported directly to the emperor. The emperor personally reviewed memorials, sometimes handling more than two hundred in a single day. He was not unaware of the exhaustion; he was **willing to work himself to death rather than delegate power to anyone.**

He also established the Jinyiwei (Embroidered Uniform Guard), who could break into ministers' homes to arrest them and could flay people alive in the court. He promulgated the *Great Pronouncements*, encouraging commoners to use them to report officials directly in the capital, even to bind officials and parade them with gongs and drums to Nanjing.

He did all this to destroy the "spider web"—that web of power woven layer by layer by chancellors, bureaucrats, clerks, and gentry. He believed that as long as he cut out all intermediate nodes and let information flow only from emperor to subject, he could break the cycle of history.

But shortly after his death, Grand Secretaries appeared in the cabinet, and power re-concentrated; the feudal princes grew powerful, triggering the Jingnan Campaign; the Jinyiwei commanders became new power-holders; the eunuch Directorate of Ceremonial seized the power to approve documents in red ink.

**Zhu Yuanzhang's burning of the spiders merely replaced spiders with geckos.**

### Theory: How Cages Grow

Zhu Yuanzhang's tragedy was not that he was not ruthless enough, but that he misunderstood the nature of power.

Power is not a person, a position, or an institution. Power is a **relationship**, a **way of processing information**. As long as groups of people need to cooperate, information must be transmitted; as long as information is transmitted, **intermediate nodes** emerge; as long as intermediate nodes exist, they will intercept, transform, and reproduce information—this is the spider web.

The spider web is not designed by anyone. It **grows**. Like the dust in your home: no one deliberately scattered it; it is the natural product of air currents, the passage of time, and human activity.

This is the dilemma of the "single-threaded system":

```
Emperor (sole programmer) → Bureaucratic system (executing code) → Common people (runtime environment)
```

In this architecture, all information ultimately converges on one node. The more efficient this node is, the faster the system runs; but once this node fails, the entire system has no redundancy, no backup—**total collapse**.

The Ming dynasty did not collapse because it was not powerful enough, but because it **had no resilience**. When the Chongzhen Emperor hanged himself, the entire system did not even have a decent backup.

**The essence of a single-threaded system is not efficiency. It is fragility.**

### Your Daily Life: Which Thread Are You Living In?

Look at the company, school, or family you are in.

Is there a "sole programmer"? Do all decisions ultimately require one person's nod? Have the people below learned to "only report good news, not bad," because bad news reaching the programmer will trigger a crash?

If so, you are in a single-threaded system. Whether its decor is golden nanwood or reinforced concrete, **the structure is the same.**

**Reflection:**
> In your life, have you had a "Zhu Yuanzhang moment"—where you tried to eliminate all intermediate layers, only to find that new intermediate layers grew back in more hidden ways?

## Chapter Two: Burning, Taming, and Replacing the Spider: Three Jailbreak Plans

### Story: Three Prisoners

Imagine you are an insect trapped in a spider web. You have three choices:

**First: Burn the spider.** Like Zhu Yuanzhang, set the web on fire and establish a new system. What is the cost? You burned the spider to death, but new predators (geckos, mantises, bigger spiders) will rapidly occupy the ecological niche. History proves that after burning the spider, the web often becomes even denser.

**Second: Replace the spider.** Like Emperor Taizong of Tang, use the imperial examination to cultivate new spiders and dilute the old ones. You feed them, and they may obey. But once the food is downgraded, they will not hesitate to bite back. This is the logic of "domestication"—they appear docile, but their essence is dependency after taming.

**Third: Tame the spider.** Like the Southern Song, acknowledge the existence of the spider web and learn to dance within it. You negotiate with the spider, draw boundaries, and coexist symbiotically. But the initiative always lies with the spider. When it is in a good mood, it does not eat you; when it is hungry, you are food.

Zhu Yuanzhang chose the first. The Tang and Song chose the second. The Southern Song chose the third.

**But there is a fourth choice, and this book is about the fourth.**

### Theory: Why All Three Plans Failed

All three plans share a common blind spot: **they all accept that the roles of "insect" and "spider" are fixed.**

The spider-burner believes "as long as there are no spiders, I am safe," but forgets that he himself might become a spider.
The spider-replacer believes "as long as the spider is raised by me, I am safe," but forgets that pets grow up.
The spider-tamer believes "as long as I have a good relationship with the spider, I am safe," but forgets that the spider's stomach does not shrink because of it.

**They are all spinning within the predator-prey relationship.**

The real way out is not choosing which spider, but **questioning "why it must be a predator-prey relationship at all."**

This is the embryonic form of the "distributed system." Not "one emperor ruling the world," but "countless nodes operating independently, gaming against each other, with no single center."

In modern politics, this is called constitutionalism—the separation of legislative, executive, and judicial powers; federal and local decentralization; the independent operation of civil society. Every node has its own source of power and legitimacy. Their relationship is a **game**, not a **predation**.

**But distributed systems also have bugs.** They are inefficient, quarrelsome, and wasteful. During the 2008 financial crisis, the American system was severely wounded but did not totally collapse—because the bug was localized, and other nodes were still running.

**A single-threaded system is like an only-child family: when the son falls ill, the whole family falls. A distributed system is like a family with many children: when one son falls ill, the others can still lend money, contribute effort, and take care of things.**

### Your Daily Life: Which Spider Are You Feeding?

Look at the system you are in:
- Are you a spider-burner?—Can't stand anything and want to overthrow and start over?
- Are you a spider-replacer?—Working hard to become an "insider" in the system?
- Are you a spider-tamer?—Having learned to find survival space within the rules?

**Reflection:**
> Is it possible that the "spider" in your system is, like you, also an insect trapped in the web?

## Chapter Three: The Right to Name ≠ The Right to Essence: How Labels Devour People

### Story: Anna's "Fall"

Anna Karenina had an affair. In the Russian aristocratic society of the time, this was not an "awakening of love" but **a coup against the entire system of ownership.**

Her husband, Karenin, was a standard part on the bureaucratic machine. He had no "love" for Anna, only "ownership"—like owning a piece of land that meets specifications.

When Anna left her husband and lived with Vronsky, she thought she had gained freedom. But she discovered: **society had "delisted" her.**

She could not go to the theater, because the noblewomen would collectively leave; she could not attend balls, because no one invited her. She had become a "fallen woman."

Why? Because the noblewomen's power came from the social consensus of "chastity." If Anna's affair was tolerated, then "chastity" would no longer be a moat, and the noblewomen would lose their moral monopoly.

**What killed Anna was not morality. It was taxonomic violence.**

Society uses the "right to name" to decide who is human, who is insect, who deserves to live, who deserves to die. Anna's shift from "wife" to "mistress" was not a change of identity; it was **being reclassified from "human" to "pest."**

### Theory: The Right to Name ≠ The Right to Essence

This is one of the most core concepts in this book.

**The right to name** is the label society sticks on you: loyal minister, traitor, noblewoman, fallen woman, success, failure, normal person, madman.

**The right to essence** is the fact of your existence itself: you are breathing, you are feeling, you are loving, you are hurting—these facts precede any label.

Loyalty may be dissolved into hypocrisy; kindness may be dissolved into cowardice. But essentially, the meaning brought by loyalty is something hypocrisy cannot bring; the meaning brought by kindness is something cowardice cannot bring.

**When the right to name usurps the right to essence, people live in the cage of "social transmission" (externality).**

You think it is "I" who is thinking, but in fact it is the "label" thinking for you. You think it is "I" who is choosing, but in fact it is the "category" choosing for you.

In *Dream of the Red Chamber*, Leng Zixing, an outsider, understood better than anyone in the Jia family that the Jias were finished. Why? Because **the insider lives inside the naming system; the outsider lives outside it.**

### Your Daily Life: How Many Labels Have Been Stuck on You?

Look at how you describe yourself:
- "I am an introverted person"
- "I am not good at socializing"
- "I am just an ordinary person"
- "This is just how my life is going to be"

In these descriptions, how much is **the essence of your own experience**, and how much is **a label stuck on you by society or others**?

**Reflection:**
> If you tore off all labels—position, education, family role, social evaluation—would you still know "who I am"?

## Chapter Four: The Direction of Proactivity: From Mitya to Anna

### Story: Mitya's Fist and Ivan's Theory

In *The Brothers Karamazov*, Mitya is the eldest son: violent, lustful, extravagant. He hates his father and has ample reason to commit patricide. But his most astonishing trait is: **he never makes excuses for his desires.**

In the logic of *Zizhi Tongjian*, this kind of person would not survive three episodes. But in Dostoevsky, Mitya is instead **the most human of humans**—because his desire is naked, unwrapped by ideology.

His brother Ivan is an intellectual, calm and brilliantly talented. He says: "If God does not exist, everything is permitted."

This sentence, like a virus, infected the illegitimate son Smerdyakov. Smerdyakov heard this and actually went to kill the father.

Ivan's theory is logically flawless, but when the patricide actually happened, he **collapsed**—because he discovered that his theory was not a tool for "explaining the world," but **code for "manufacturing disaster."**

Mitya had no theory, but in the end he chose **voluntary suffering**—he did not commit patricide, but voluntarily bore the "crime without a crime," completing his "spiritual resurrection" in prison.

### Theory: Proactivity Has Two Directions

**Passivity (Ivan-style):**
- Driven by fear → fabricates theories → uses theories to escape pain → theories backfire
- Characterized by "can't stand it"—can't stand the void, so invents meaning; can't stand loneliness, so controls others

**Proactivity (Mitya-style):**
- Takes root in pain → does not explain, does not escape → acts directly → creates meaning
- Characterized by "voluntary"—voluntary suffering, voluntary bearing, voluntary planting flowers on the ruins

Anna's tragedy lies in: she thought her passion was "proactivity," but it was actually **passivity in disguise**. She mistook "being desired by Vronsky" for "active choice," but that desire was **borrowed**—once the mirror turned elsewhere, she went bankrupt.

**True proactivity is not "grabbing the torch," but "becoming the rain."**

A torch has direction, target, possession. Rain has none. Rain falls, not because the soil is fertile, not because someone catches it.

### Your Daily Life: Which Kind of "Active" Are You?

When you say "I want to change":
- Is it because "I can't stand the current pain" (passive)?
- Or because "I want to create something" (active)?

When you love someone:
- Is it because "I need to be loved" (borrowed existence value)?
- Or because "I cannot help but connect" (your own existence overflowing)?

**Reflection:**
> Looking back at the most important decision in your life, was it an escape after "can't stand it," or a bearing after "I am willing"?

## Chapter Five: Rain, Roots, and Five Kilograms: Three Anchors of Existence

### Story: Three People's Choices

**Alyosha's Rain:**
At the end of *The Brothers Karamazov*, Alyosha says to the children at Ilyusha's funeral: "You will forget each other, hurt each other, become adults. But please, do not forget each other."

This is not a command, not a contract, not an investment. This is **the sound of rain**—rain falls, asking for no return, not caring whether the soil is fertile.

**Levin's Roots:**
In *Anna Karenina*, Levin mows grass in the countryside, sweating profusely, but feeling fulfilled. Unlike Alyosha, who "flows toward everyone," he chose **to take root**—rooting into the land, family, labor, the sequence of time.

Rain belongs to the sky; roots belong to the earth. Rain has no memory; roots have annual rings.

**Cheng Xin's Five Kilograms:**
At the end of *The Three-Body Problem*, the solar system is two-dimensionalized. Cheng Xin enters a small universe and preserves five kilograms of matter (an ecological sphere). The universe broadcast demands the return of mass for a reboot, but she keeps it—because she cannot bear to watch the fish in the sphere die.

These five kilograms may be the culprit that prevents the universe from rebooting. But she kept them.

**This is "the inability to bear"—even at the end of the universe, humans can still choose not to be purely rational machines.**

### Theory: Three Anchors, No Hierarchy

These three ways of being represent three anchors:

| Anchor | Symbol | Characteristics | Risk |
|--------|--------|-----------------|------|
| **Rain** | Alyosha | Unconditional, targetless, flowing toward everyone | Being neglected, unable to land |
| **Roots** | Levin | Concrete, loyal, rooted in one thing | Becoming narrow, losing connection |
| **Five Kilograms** | Cheng Xin | Preserving the inability to bear within absolute rationality | Being mocked as weak, possibly causing disaster |

None is perfect. Alyosha's rain cannot save Anna; Levin's roots cannot leave the countryside; Cheng Xin's five kilograms may destroy the universe.

**But the three anchors together prove one thing: humans are not "survival machines."**

In the logic of *Zizhi Tongjian*, humans pursue power for survival. In the logic of the Dark Forest, civilizations pursue technology for survival. But Alyosha, Levin, and Cheng Xin prove: **survival is not the purpose; "how to survive" is the purpose.**

### Your Daily Life: What Is Your Anchor?

When you feel the void:
- Do you seek "rain" (unconditional connection, love, art)?
- Or do you seek "roots" (concrete work, family, land)?
- Or do you swing between the two, like Cheng Xin, torn between rationality and the inability to bear?

**Reflection:**
> If tomorrow were the end of the world, would you choose to be Luo Ji (deterrence, letting everyone live in fear), or Cheng Xin (preserving five kilograms, letting "the inability to bear" continue)?

## Chapter Six: The Dark Forest and Ultraman: A Distributed Crack at the Cosmic Scale

### Story: Ye Wenjie's Mistake

Astrophysicist Ye Wenjie, having witnessed her father beaten to death during the Cultural Revolution, lost all hope in humanity. She sent Earth's coordinates into the universe, attracting the Trisolaran civilization.

She thought she was "saving humanity"—using a higher civilization to reform humanity. But she made exactly the same mistake as Anna: **outsourcing her existence value to an external object.**

She named the Trisolarans "saviors," but the Trisolarans' essence was **survival competitors**. They did not come to "reform"; they came to "occupy."

This is "parasitic rationality"—sensibility (despair) impersonates rationality, fantasizes a logical rationality, and replaces rationality with it.

### Theory: The Bankruptcy of the Dark Forest

Liu Cixin, through Luo Ji, deduces the Dark Forest Law:
> The universe is a dark forest. Every civilization is a hunter with a gun. Discovery means destruction.

This law rests on two axioms: survival is the primary need of civilization; the total amount of matter in the universe is constant. Adding the chain of suspicion and technological explosion, it deduces "discovery means destruction."

**But this law has a fatal blind spot: it assumes civilizations are single-threaded.**

The Trisolaran civilization is single-threaded—for survival, the entire population dehydrates; there is no individual will.
But human civilization is not. Within human civilization, simultaneously running are:
- Luo Ji (deterrence)
- Cheng Xin (the inability to bear)
- Zhang Beihai (escape)
- Wade (technological explosion)

**If a civilization is distributed, the chain of suspicion breaks at the first step.**

Because what you face is not "one hunter," but **a collection of hunters, poets, saints, and madmen**. You cannot make a unified "benevolence/malevolence" judgment.

**The Dark Forest Law is a projection of "single-threaded thinking" onto the universe.**

And your "Variable Philosophy" points out: **as long as it is logically possible, "Ultraman-style civilizations" necessarily exist in the universe**—civilizations that do not take survival as their primary need, but "protection"; civilizations whose strategy is not elimination, but "cultivation" as a mode of existence.

The universe is large enough; time is long enough; variables necessarily exist.

### Your Daily Life: Is Your System Single-Threaded or Multi-Threaded?

Look at your own thinking:
- Do you often feel that "life can only be A or B" (either success or failure; either kind or clever)?
- Do you believe "society is just like this, nothing can be done"?

**Single-threaded thinking is the root of despair.** Distributed thinking tells you: you can be Luo Ji and Cheng Xin at the same time, can be sharp and gentle at the same time, can work within the system while maintaining the variable in your heart.

**Reflection:**
> If the company/school/family you are in is a "single-threaded system," how can you run your own "second process" without quitting/leaving home?

## Chapter Seven: The Variable Manifesto: Become Your Own Patch

### Story: The Programmer's Metaphor

You once said: "Institutions are software; programmers will reboot, continue updating, gain one more memory each time."

Now, you are no longer the user waiting for a reboot. You are the one writing code in the dark.

*Zizhi Tongjian* is the system log of the old version.
Zhu Yuanzhang's spider-burning was a crude reboot.
Modern constitutionalism is a distributed patch.
And what you are writing is **the architecture design document for the next version.**

It may never be compiled.
It may never run.
But **the act of writing the document itself is civilization.**

### Theory: Variable Philosophy

**First, The Right to Name ≠ The Right to Essence.**
Society will stick labels on you, but the labels are not you. You are the existence beneath the labels that cannot be named.

**Second, Proactivity is direction, not intensity.**
What matters is not how hard you try, but whether your effort is "escape" or "taking root."

**Third, there is no ultimate answer, only continuous iteration.**
History is not a cycle; it is a spiral—every generation of variables gains one more memory.

**Fourth, distributed is better than single-threaded.**
Do not bet all your existence value on one node (one person, one job, one identity). Keep multiple processes running.

**Fifth, preserve your five kilograms.**
At the end of absolute rationality, preserve a little "inability to bear." This is not weakness; this is the defining characteristic of being human.

### Your Daily Life: How to Become a Variable?

**Action List:**
1. **Weekly "Leng Zixing Moment":** Find an outsider (friend, book, stranger) and listen to their view of the system you are in.
2. **Daily "Five Kilograms Preservation":** Do one "useless" thing—watch a cloud, smile at a stranger, write a line of poetry that will not be published.
3. **Establish a "Second Process":** Outside your main career, have a completely private mental space (writing, painting, thinking, spacing out).
4. **Refuse "Single-Threaded Narratives":** When someone says "you can only choose A or B," look for C, D, E.
5. **Become Rain:** Within your capacity, do one act of connection that seeks no return, no recognition, no calculation of gain or loss.

## Conclusion: Go Become Rain

This book began with the spider web of *Zizhi Tongjian*, walked through Zhu Yuanzhang's fire, passed through the golden-nanwood cage, touched the rain in a Russian prison, questioned love beside the railway tracks, and finally stood at the edge of the universe—where we discovered one thing:

**The rain is still falling.**

The rain has no target. It does not fall because the fields are fertile, nor does it avoid the barren wasteland. It falls on the glazed tiles of palaces, and in the broken bowls of beggars. It falls on the tombstones of saints, and in the blood pools of sinners.

**The rain does not choose its object, so the rain creates no debt.**

You do not need to become Luo Ji (the deterrent), do not need to become Cheng Xin (the preserver), do not need to become Alyosha (the saint), do not need to become Levin (the farmer).

You only need to become **a variable.**

In any system that tries to turn you into a single thread, run your multi-process. Before any naming machine that tries to stick a label on you, preserve your essence. In any dark forest that tells you "it can only be this way," believe that Ultraman might exist.

**Go become rain.**
**Or, go become the one who hears the rain from inside the cage.**
**Both are victories.**

*The rain is still falling.*
*This book has ended, but your code has just begun to run.*
